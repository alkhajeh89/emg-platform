> **Revision 1 — Draft for Architecture Board review.** This document is not
> Accepted and authorizes no implementation. It defines the reconciliation and
> delivery contract that ADR-027 Revision 5 and ADR-030 Revision 4 both defer to
> this ADR. It amends no accepted ADR, creates no mutation route, command, or
> DTO, and changes no product scope. Every statement is labelled **Fact**,
> **Decision**, **Consequence**, or **Open Question**. Where repository evidence
> is insufficient, an Open Question is recorded instead of an invented answer.

## 1. Context

**Fact.** ADR-027 Revision 5 §5A defines exactly five mutation routes. Stage 4
is complete and Stage 5 deployment documentation exists. Mutation audit
*intent* generation is owned by ADR-027; `EMG_ADR-027_KNOWLEDGE_GRAPH_MUTATION_API.md:1494`
assigns "Audit reconciliation" to "ADR-028 (future)" with the note "Define
mutation audit intent only" and classifies it a "Deferred production-enablement
dependency".

**Fact.** `EMG_ADR-027_KNOWLEDGE_GRAPH_MUTATION_API.md:1319` states that ADR-028
"must account for mutation-sourced audit events (§9.4, §13) alongside read-path
and operation-level events."

**Fact.** `EMG_ADR-027_KNOWLEDGE_GRAPH_MUTATION_API.md:832` states that for
Delete, Restore, Merge, and Reclassify, "Their audit actions remain owned by
this Mutation API and ADR-028's future reconciliation work".

**Fact.** ADR-030 Revision 4 lists ADR-028 as "(Audit reconciliation, when
approved)" (`:34`) and states in §5.4 that `mutation_dispatch` "is a foundation
only; ADR-030 does not implement a publisher or audit worker."

**Fact.** `services/audit` is implemented (EPIC-04, FEAT-04-1→4) and exposes
`POST /events`, `GET /events`, `GET /events/page`, `GET /events/export`,
`GET /integrity`, and five chain-of-custody routes.

**Fact.** ADR-034 is Accepted and implemented. It assigns `tenant_id` from the
verified producer token, includes it in version-3 audit-event hashing, and
applies mandatory server-side tenant and classification constraints to every
audit read.

**Fact.** `docs/architecture/EMG_PRODUCTION_READINESS_ROADMAP.md:91` records
ADR-028 as "Referenced, not written, not begun" and as blocking "any
cross-service audit-integrity guarantee beyond single-service append-only logs."

## 2. Problem Statement

**Fact.** Mutation audit intents are durably stored and never delivered.

- `mutation_ledger.audit_intents` is `JSONB, not null` and holds the "Exact
  immutable `MutationAuditIntent` collection generated by the application for
  the committed mutation" (ADR-030 `:214`).
- ADR-030 `:362` states `audit_intents` "is the canonical internal audit handoff
  and must equal the audit-intent member stored in `mutation_result`."
- ADR-030 §16 (`:1304`) lists "**Audit persistence:** project stored
  `audit_intents` to the existing audit contract using deterministic event
  identities derived from `mutation_id`" under **Future Compatibility** — a
  compatibility statement, not an obligation, and unowned.
- Repository search for consumers of `audit_intents` returns the ledger model,
  the mutation repository, `atomic_mutation.py`, `service.py`, and six test
  modules. **No component projects them to `services/audit`.**

**Fact.** No accepted cross-service audit reconciliation contract exists. No
accepted ADR defines how mutation-sourced events are reconciled with read-path
and operation-level audit events.

**Consequence.** Every committed Knowledge Graph mutation produces audit intent
that is durably retained and externally invisible. The audit service's
append-only log is complete for what it receives and silent about mutations.

## 3. Decision

**Decision D-1.** ADR-028 owns **reconciliation and delivery of stored mutation
audit intents into the audit service**, and owns nothing else. It does not
generate audit intent, does not store it, and does not change how audit reads
are confined.

**Decision D-2.** Delivery consumes the existing `mutation_dispatch` foundation
on channel `audit` (ADR-030 §5.4, V004 `:123-158`) and the existing audit
producer contract `POST /events`. **No new transport, table, or ledger column is
introduced by this ADR.**

**Decision D-3.** The projector is a **consumer** of ADR-030's foundation and a
**producer** to the audit service. It sits outside both. It never writes to
`mutation_ledger`, never mutates the graph, and never bypasses the audit
service's own ingestion path.

**Decision D-4.** Delivery is **at-least-once with idempotent acceptance**. The
audit service already treats a repeated `event_id` as a duplicate and does not
append a second record (`emg_audit_client/event.py:65-68`). ADR-028 relies on
that existing property rather than introducing exactly-once semantics.

**Decision D-5.** ADR-028 introduces **no change to authorization semantics**.
The projector authenticates to the audit service as an ordinary service
principal under the existing ADR-034 trust model.

## 4. Scope

**Decision.** The following are within ADR-028:

1. **Deterministic projection** of stored `MutationAuditIntent` values into the
   `SubmittedAuditEvent` producer contract.
2. **Idempotent delivery** using a deterministic, reproducible `event_id`.
3. **Tenant-preserving and classification-preserving mapping**, subject to
   Open Question OQ-1.
4. **Reconciliation** of mutation-sourced events with read-path and
   operation-level audit events, as ADR-027 `:1319` requires.
5. **Retry, replay, failure, and recovery semantics** for the delivery path.
6. **Evidence and acceptance criteria** sufficient for an implementation review.

## 5. Out of Scope

**Decision.** The following are explicitly outside ADR-028 and are not authorized
by it:

- New mutation routes or DTOs. The five-route ADR-027 Revision 5 surface is
  unchanged.
- Ledger schema changes. Any new `mutation_ledger` or `mutation_dispatch`
  column requires a separate ADR-030 revision (ADR-030 §4.4).
- Any user interface.
- Production observability backend, dashboards, tracing, or alerting
  (ADR-015 / FEAT-12-3).
- T-A-002 supported-schema discovery.
- T-A-003 durable effective-schema-version recording.
- ADR-033 Revision 2 Phase 4.
- Any change to the Product Architecture Freeze.
- Any change to existing authorization semantics, including ADR-025 policy
  rules, ADR-026 classification enforcement, and ADR-034 audit read
  confinement.
- Physical erasure, which ADR-027 §9.3 keeps out of scope platform-wide.

## 6. Architecture

**Fact.** The delivery substrate already exists. `V004__mutation_ledger.sql:123-158`
creates `mutation_dispatch` with primary key `(channel, mutation_id)`, a
`channel` check constraint permitting `'audit'` and `'event'`, `tenant_id`,
`available_at`, `attempt_count`, `claim_owner`, `claim_expires_at`,
`delivered_at`, and four nullable source-position columns. Index
`ix_mutation_dispatch_work` covers `(channel, available_at, mutation_id) WHERE
delivered_at IS NULL`.

**Fact.** ADR-030 §5.4 specifies the claim protocol: work-set based polling, row
locks with skip-locked semantics, transactional claim and delivery transitions,
expired claims becoming eligible for another worker, delivery idempotent using
`(channel, mutation_id)` as the external event identity, and rows remaining
queryable until delivery is durably acknowledged.

**Decision D-6.** The reconciliation architecture is a single logical flow with
no new persistent state:

```
mutation_ledger.audit_intents   (ADR-030, immutable, already written)
            |
            |  read-only
            v
mutation_dispatch (channel='audit')   (ADR-030 section 5.4 foundation, exists)
            |
            |  claim -> project -> deliver -> acknowledge
            v
Audit Projector   (ADR-028 — the only new component)
            |
            |  POST /events, one call per projected event
            v
services/audit    (EPIC-04, unchanged; ADR-034 confinement unchanged)
```

**Decision D-7.** Ordering guarantee is **per-mutation, not global**. Within one
`mutation_id`, projected events are delivered in the stored `audit_intents`
collection order. Across mutations, no total order is asserted.

**Fact.** ADR-030 §5.4 states "transaction commit reordering cannot cause a
mutation to be skipped" because polling searches the remaining work set. This
supports completeness, not global ordering.

**Consequence.** Consumers requiring a total order must use the audit service's
own `sequence_number`, which the audit store assigns on append.

## 7. Components

**Decision D-8.** One new component: the **Audit Projector**.

| Responsibility | In ADR-028 |
| :--- | :--- |
| Claim undelivered `mutation_dispatch` rows on channel `audit` | Yes |
| Read `mutation_ledger.audit_intents` for the claimed `mutation_id` | Yes |
| Derive a deterministic `event_id` per intent | Yes |
| Map `MutationAuditIntent` to `SubmittedAuditEvent` | Yes |
| Deliver via the existing audit producer contract | Yes |
| Acknowledge delivery by setting `delivered_at` | Yes |
| Generate audit intent | **No — ADR-027** |
| Store audit intent | **No — ADR-030** |
| Confine audit reads | **No — ADR-034** |
| Publish graph-revision events | **No — existing revision outbox** |

**Open Question OQ-5** records where the projector is deployed.

**Fact — field inventory.** `MutationAuditIntent`
(`services/knowledge-graph/src/emg_knowledge_graph/results.py:39-56`) carries:
`tenant`, `principal`, `idempotency_key`, `action`, `resource_type`,
`resource_id`, `related_resource_ids`, `classification`, `reason`,
`revision_number`, `content_hash`.

**Fact.** `SubmittedAuditEvent`
(`libs/python/emg-audit-client/src/emg_audit_client/event.py:60-82`) requires:
`event_id`, `actor`, `actor_type`, `module`, `action`, `outcome`, and optionally
`correlation_id`, `resource_type`, `resource_id`, `policy_id`. The audit router
(`services/audit/src/emg_audit_service/routers/events.py:60-66`) additionally
reads `classification`, `source_system`, and `reason` from the submitted event.

**Consequence.** Four ADR-030-preserved facts — `idempotency_key`,
`related_resource_ids`, `revision_number`, and `content_hash` — have no
destination in the current audit contract. They remain durably available in the
ledger. **OQ-6** records whether reconciliation requires them to be delivered.

### 7.1 Event identity derivation

**Fact.** `mutation_dispatch` primary key is `(channel, mutation_id)` — one row
per mutation per channel. `mutation_ledger.audit_intents` is a **collection**.
One dispatch row therefore corresponds to one or more audit events.

**Fact.** ADR-030 §16 specifies "deterministic event identities derived from
`mutation_id`", and §5.4 describes `(channel, mutation_id)` as "the external
event identity" for *dispatch* idempotency.

**Decision D-9.** The projected `event_id` is derived deterministically from the
`mutation_id` together with the intent's **zero-based ordinal position** in the
stored `audit_intents` collection. Two properties follow:

1. **Reproducible.** The same ledger row always yields the same identities. The
   ledger is immutable (ADR-030 §4.4), so the collection order is stable.
2. **Distinct per intent.** A multi-intent mutation produces distinct
   `event_id` values, satisfying the audit service's per-`event_id` duplicate
   rule.

**Decision D-10.** The exact derivation function is a normative contract this
ADR fixes in principle and leaves to implementation review to express. It must
be pure, stable across processes and releases, and must not depend on wall-clock
time, hostname, or process identity.

## 8. Integration with ADR-027

**Fact.** ADR-027 Revision 5 owns mutation audit **intent generation**
(`:1494`). ADR-027 `:832` states audit actions for Delete, Restore, Merge, and
Reclassify "remain owned by this Mutation API and ADR-028's future
reconciliation work".

**Decision D-11.** ADR-028 consumes `MutationAuditIntent` **as ADR-027 produces
it**. It requests no additional field and proposes no change to intent
generation, to the five routes, to any command, or to any DTO.

**Decision D-12.** ADR-028 satisfies ADR-027 `:1319` — "must account for
mutation-sourced audit events alongside read-path and operation-level events" —
by delivering mutation-sourced events into the **same** audit store and through
the **same** producer contract that read-path and operation-level events already
use. Reconciliation is achieved by convergence on one store, not by a second
audit surface.

**Consequence.** No ADR-027 revision is required by this ADR.

## 9. Integration with ADR-030

**Fact.** ADR-030 §4.2 scopes itself to "the durable mutation record,
fingerprint contract, replay contract, and the transaction composition required
to make idempotency atomic with GraphStore."

**Fact.** ADR-030 §5.4 states `mutation_dispatch` "is a foundation only; ADR-030
does not implement a publisher or audit worker."

**Decision D-13.** ADR-028 implements the audit worker ADR-030 declined to
implement, using ADR-030's foundation **unchanged**. It adds no column, no
index, and no constraint.

**Decision D-14.** ADR-028 reads `mutation_ledger` and never writes to it. It
writes only the mutable operational fields of `mutation_dispatch` that ADR-030
§5.4 already defines: `attempt_count`, `claim_owner`, `claim_expires_at`, and
`delivered_at`.

**Decision D-15.** The ledger's immutability (ADR-030 §4.4) is preserved. Any
future need to record reconciliation state **inside** the ledger requires an
ADR-030 revision and is out of scope here.

**Consequence.** No ADR-030 revision is required by this ADR.

## 10. Integration with ADR-034

**Fact.** ADR-034 states the audit service "assigns `tenant_id` from the verified
producer token and includes it in version-3 audit-event hashing."

**Fact.** `services/audit/src/emg_audit_service/routers/events.py:76-82` records
that "source_principal is assigned from the authenticated token, never from
producer content (Sprint 6 security-review fix, Priority 5)", and calls
`store.append(event, source_principal=principal.client_id, tenant_id=principal.tenant_id)`.

**Fact.** ADR-034 assigns legacy rows the reserved `legacy-unscoped` tenant,
"invisible to normal tenant tokens."

**Consequence — the central unresolved tension.** The audit service derives
`tenant_id` from **the producer's own token**, not from event content. A
projector delivering events for many tenants under one service token would
attribute every event to that token's tenant. `MutationAuditIntent.tenant`
carries the correct tenant and has no target field.

**Decision D-16.** ADR-028 **does not weaken** ADR-034's confinement. It does not
propose that the audit service accept a tenant from producer content, and it
does not propose a bypass of the ingestion path.

**Open Question OQ-1** records the resolution options. This ADR does not choose
among them, because each has consequences for ADR-034's accepted model and the
choice is the Board's.

**Decision D-17.** Classification is preserved by direct mapping:
`MutationAuditIntent.classification` maps to the submitted event's
`classification`, which ADR-034's read confinement then filters on. No
classification is downgraded, defaulted, or omitted in projection.

## 11. Security

**Decision D-18.** The projector authenticates to the audit service as an
ordinary service principal. It introduces no new authentication mechanism, no
new trust relationship, and no new token claim.

**Decision D-19.** The projector holds **read-only** access to `mutation_ledger`
and write access limited to the four mutable `mutation_dispatch` operational
fields. It requires no graph write access, no schema ownership, and no DDL
capability, consistent with ADR-034's `emg_knowledge_graph_app` least-privilege
model.

**Decision D-20.** Projected events carry no content beyond the fields
`MutationAuditIntent` already contains. The projector performs no enrichment
from the graph, from another tenant's data, or from any source outside the
claimed ledger row.

**Consequence.** A compromised projector can replay or delay audit events. It
cannot forge graph state, alter the immutable ledger, or widen a classification.

**Open Question OQ-7** records whether the projector requires a dedicated
registered service client.

## 12. Failure Handling

**Decision D-21 — failure and retry model.** Delivery is at-least-once. A failed
delivery leaves `delivered_at` null, so the row remains in the work set (ADR-030
§5.4: "a row remains queryable until delivery is durably acknowledged"). The
existing `attempt_count` column bounds retries. An expired claim becomes
eligible for another worker, as ADR-030 §5.4 already specifies.

**Decision D-22 — partial delivery within one mutation.** A multi-intent
mutation may fail after delivering some of its events. Because `event_id` is
deterministic (D-9), redelivery of the whole collection is safe: already-accepted
events are recognized as duplicates by the audit service and not appended twice.
**The unit of retry is the mutation, not the individual event.**

**Decision D-23 — reconciliation status model.** Reconciliation status is
expressed entirely by existing `mutation_dispatch` state, with no new column:

| Status | Representation |
| :--- | :--- |
| Pending | `delivered_at IS NULL`, no active claim |
| In flight | `delivered_at IS NULL`, `claim_owner` set, claim unexpired |
| Delivered | `delivered_at IS NOT NULL` |
| Exhausted | `delivered_at IS NULL`, `attempt_count` at the configured bound |

**Consequence.** "Exhausted" is derived, not stored. **Open Question OQ-8**
records whether an exhausted row requires an operator-visible signal beyond a
metric.

**Decision D-24.** Delivery failure never affects mutation correctness. The
mutation is already committed; ADR-030's atomicity guarantees are unchanged by
any projector outcome.

## 13. Rollback

**Decision D-25.** Rollback is a pure code and configuration revert. Stopping
the projector leaves `mutation_dispatch` rows undelivered and queryable; ADR-030
§5.4 already guarantees they are not lost. Restarting resumes from the work set.

**Decision D-26 — replay behaviour.** Replay is performed by clearing
`delivered_at` for the affected rows and allowing normal claiming to resume.
Because `event_id` is deterministic (D-9), replayed events are recognized as
duplicates and do not create second records. **Replay is therefore safe by
construction and requires no reconciliation of the audit store.**

**Consequence.** No persisted state outside `mutation_dispatch`'s mutable
operational fields depends on this ADR's mechanism.

## 14. Migration

**Decision D-27.** No database migration is required. `mutation_dispatch` exists
at V004 and already permits the `audit` channel.

**Fact.** Migrations V003 (`mutation_idempotency`), V004 (`mutation_ledger`,
`mutation_ledger_resource`, `mutation_dispatch`), and V005
(`runtime_least_privilege`) are present in
`libs/python/emg-persistence/src/emg_persistence/migrations/postgres/`.

**Open Question OQ-9** records the treatment of mutations committed before the
projector is enabled.

## 15. Acceptance Criteria

**Decision.** An implementation satisfying this ADR must demonstrate:

**Projection correctness**

- AC-1. Every stored `MutationAuditIntent` produces exactly one projected event.
- AC-2. `action`, `resource_type`, `resource_id`, `classification`, and `reason`
  are carried without alteration.
- AC-3. No projected field is defaulted, truncated, or invented beyond the
  derivations this ADR fixes.

**Identity and idempotency**

- AC-4. `event_id` derivation is pure and reproducible across processes and
  releases.
- AC-5. A multi-intent mutation produces distinct `event_id` values.
- AC-6. Redelivering a mutation creates no second audit record.
- AC-7. Clearing `delivered_at` and replaying creates no second audit record.

**Ordering and completeness**

- AC-8. Events for one `mutation_id` are delivered in stored collection order.
- AC-9. No committed mutation with dispatch rows is skipped.
- AC-10. No total ordering across mutations is asserted or relied upon.

**Failure and recovery**

- AC-11. A delivery failure leaves the row claimable and undelivered.
- AC-12. An expired claim becomes eligible for another worker.
- AC-13. Attempts are bounded by `attempt_count`.
- AC-14. Projector failure of any kind leaves mutation correctness unaffected.

**Boundaries**

- AC-15. `mutation_ledger` is never written by the projector.
- AC-16. Only `attempt_count`, `claim_owner`, `claim_expires_at`, and
  `delivered_at` are written on `mutation_dispatch`.
- AC-17. No mutation route, command, or DTO changes.
- AC-18. No ledger or dispatch schema change occurs.
- AC-19. ADR-034 audit read confinement is unchanged by this path.
- AC-20. No classification is downgraded or omitted in projection.

**Reconciliation**

- AC-21. Mutation-sourced events are retrievable through the existing audit
  read API alongside read-path and operation-level events, satisfying
  ADR-027 `:1319`.

## 16. Consequences

**Positive**

- The gap ADR-030 §16 identified and left unowned is closed by a named decision.
- ADR-027 `:1319`'s standing requirement on ADR-028 becomes satisfiable.
- The roadmap blocker at `EMG_PRODUCTION_READINESS_ROADMAP.md:91` acquires a
  defined resolution path.
- No new table, transport, or ledger column is introduced. The design consumes
  foundations ADR-030 already built and declined to use.

**Negative**

- A new long-running component enters the platform, with its own failure,
  claim-expiry, and monitoring characteristics.
- At-least-once delivery makes correctness dependent on the audit service's
  existing `event_id` duplicate rule. Should that rule change, this design's
  idempotency guarantee changes with it.
- Four ledger facts — `idempotency_key`, `related_resource_ids`,
  `revision_number`, `content_hash` — have no destination in the current audit
  contract and are not delivered unless OQ-6 is resolved otherwise.

**Neutral**

- Reconciliation is achieved by convergence on the existing audit store rather
  than by a new reconciliation surface. Consumers gain no new API.

## 17. Open Questions

Each of the following lacks sufficient repository evidence to decide. None is
answered by inference in this draft.

**OQ-1 — Tenant attribution across the producer boundary. (Blocking)**
ADR-034 assigns `tenant_id` from the verified producer token, never from
producer content. `MutationAuditIntent.tenant` carries the authoritative tenant
and has no target field. Options include: a per-tenant projector identity; an
ADR-034-governed exception permitting a trusted projector to assert tenant; or
accepting attribution to the projector's own tenant. **Each option has
consequences for ADR-034's accepted model. The Board must choose; this ADR does
not.**

**OQ-2 — `actor_type`, `module`, and `source_system` derivation.**
All three are required or read by the audit contract and absent from
`MutationAuditIntent`. Whether they are fixed constants for the mutation channel
or derived from the principal is undecided.

**OQ-3 — `outcome` for projected events.**
`SubmittedAuditEvent.outcome` is required. ADR-030 §4.3 records only `succeeded`
and `no_op` in the ledger, and states that "Validation failures, authorization
denials, domain failures, optimistic conflicts, and infrastructure failures do
not produce ledger rows". Whether `no_op` maps to the same outcome as
`succeeded` is undecided.

**OQ-4 — Correlation identifier propagation.**
`SubmittedAuditEvent.correlation_id` is optional; `MutationAuditIntent` carries
none. Whether correlation must survive from the mutation request into the
projected event is undecided.

**OQ-5 — Projector deployment location.**
Whether the projector is a mode of an existing service, a new deployable, or a
scheduled job is undecided. `services/knowledge-graph` owns the ledger writer;
`services/audit` owns the consumer contract; neither obviously owns the bridge.

**OQ-6 — Delivery of ledger facts with no audit target field.**
Whether `idempotency_key`, `related_resource_ids`, `revision_number`, and
`content_hash` must reach the audit store is undecided. Delivering them would
require either an audit contract change or encoding into an existing field;
both exceed this ADR's stated scope.

**OQ-7 — Dedicated service client for the projector.**
Whether the projector requires its own registered client and role, in the shape
of `svc-knowledge-graph-writer`, is undecided.

**OQ-8 — Operator signal for exhausted rows.**
Whether an exhausted dispatch row requires an operator-visible signal beyond a
metric, and whether ADR-015 / FEAT-12-3 owns that signal, is undecided.

**OQ-9 — Pre-enablement backlog.**
Whether mutations committed before the projector is enabled are projected
retroactively, and over what window, is undecided. `mutation_dispatch` rows
exist for every committed mutation, so retroactive projection is mechanically
possible; whether it is desirable is a governance question.

**OQ-10 — Relationship to read-path and operation-level event volume.**
ADR-027 `:1319` requires accounting for mutation-sourced events "alongside"
read-path and operation-level events. Whether reconciliation implies any
cross-checking beyond shared storage is undecided.
